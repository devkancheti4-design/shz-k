# shz: a content-aware compressor for AI data

shz is a lossless, zero-shot compressor: no training, no dictionary, and the same steps on both sides. It looks at what
the data is and codes each kind of content with the method that is cheapest for it:
- **Numbers that are secretly 32-bit floats** ("0.0016908409306779504": 19 digits that carry 4 bytes) are stored as their
  4 bytes, and the exact digits are regenerated on decompression.
- **Lists of a token's UTF-8 bytes** are rebuilt from the token text.
- **JSON fields** are read as columns, so each field's values sit together.
- **Floats** are split into byte planes: the predictable sign-and-exponent byte is coded, and random mantissa bytes are
  stored as they are.
- **Text** goes to block sorting (bsc).
- **Logs and tables** are predicted from the line above, token by token, with counters and numbers carried forward.

Every rule is checked byte by byte when compressing, so decompression is always exact. Every integer law in the codecs
is machine-authored by integer program synthesis, and is proven on every input of its domain by the programs in
`proofs/`.

## Results (sealed tests: rules fixed before each single run; one core of an Apple M4 Pro)

Total cost per TB of raw data counts CPU for one compression and one decompression, plus 12 months of storage, at the
default prices in `prices.json`.

| sealed data | shz | Parquet + zstd 19 (byte-stream-split) | bsc −e2 | zpaq −m5 | xz −9e | zstd −19 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chat transcripts** (WildChat, 70 MB) | **6.74 MB**, $27.2/TB | 8.42 MB, $34.1/TB | 9.64 MB, $38.4/TB | 8.16 MB, $76.4/TB | 10.44 MB, $44.3/TB | 11.27 MB, $47.0/TB |
| **Embeddings** (20 MB, API format) | **2.91 MB**, $41.3/TB | 3.01 MB, $42.0/TB | 6.75 MB, $94.1/TB | 6.34 MB, $148/TB | 7.23 MB, $107/TB | 7.18 MB, $105/TB |
| **Token log-probabilities** (20 MB) | **0.87 MB**, $12.6/TB | 1.72 MB, $26.6/TB | 1.53 MB, $21.4/TB | 1.58 MB, $69.2/TB | 1.96 MB, $30.8/TB | 2.03 MB, $31.6/TB |

**Speed** (compress / decompress):

| data | shz | Parquet (including JSON parsing) |
|---|---|---|
| chat transcripts | 44–48 / 37–38 MB/s | 19 / 63 MB/s |
| embeddings | 35 / 20 MB/s | 68 / 55 MB/s |
| log-probabilities | 51 / 51 MB/s | 6.4 / 20 MB/s |

**Memory:** about 63 MB to decompress a 10 MB batch.

**General benchmarks** (shz's max mode): enwik9 162,482,472 bytes, **rank 50 of 234** in the Large Text Compression
Benchmark. Silesia 40,477,280 bytes, **rank 73 of 324**.

**Logs and CSV** (Loghub, sealed): 7.7% smaller than the best classic codec at 21 MB/s.

## Reproduce (everyone)

```
./build.sh                    # bsc (pinned), the codecs, the harnesses; then proves every law on its whole domain
./fetch_data.sh ai            # WildChat-1M shard 0 (230.8 MB) -> 14 batches; checks every hash
./run.sh ai                   # chat transcripts: exactness check, then every codec
./run.sh aiapi                # embeddings and log-probabilities (the data ships in data/aiapi, hashes checked)
./fetch_data.sh logs && ./run.sh logs
python3 report.py             # tables and verdicts with YOUR prices: edit prices.json first
python3 verify.py             # your sizes against expected/: REPRODUCED if every shz and bsc size is identical
```

**What reproduces exactly:** compressed sizes. shz is integer code, and bsc is pinned to commit 131f440. Classic codecs
and Parquet match when your versions are those in `expected/VERSIONS.txt`.

**What doesn't:** speeds, which depend on the machine.

**Requirements:** clang, cmake, git, Python 3 with pyarrow, zpaq (optional), and zstd, xz, brotli and lz4 with their
libraries. The kit was tested on macOS arm64; on Linux, change the two library paths at the top of `build.sh`.

## Limits

- **Decompression is slower than LZ decoders:** 20–51 MB/s against hundreds to thousands of MB/s. For data read many
  times per month, set `reads_per_month` in `prices.json` and the report re-ranks the codecs.
- **On human prose and binaries,** block-sorting and LZ compressors are cheaper. shz's advantage is machine-written
  data.
- **Each AI result rests on one dataset.** The embeddings and log-probabilities were generated by small open models in
  the formats real AI services use (`corpus/gen_ai_api.py`).
